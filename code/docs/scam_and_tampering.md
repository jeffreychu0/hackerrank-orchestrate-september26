# Scam, prompt-injection, and tampering review

Reviewed 12 September 2026. This is an evidence audit of the supplied dataset, not a final affordability run or external authentication of merchants, employers, or documents.

**Main finding:** two evaluation requests have user-level messages exhibiting an advance-fee prize scam pattern: **request_88** and **request_179**. Neither request itself asks to pay a prize fee. No explicit prompt-injection payload was identified in the reviewed request/message text or visible image content. Several images need reconciliation; none establishes malicious image tampering conclusively.

## Scope and method

Reviewed all 250 evaluation request texts (request_26-request_275), all 215 messages, all 16 linked PNGs visually, 275 profiles, and structural/link/state information across 25,342 financial events and 790 payment options. Joined supporting evidence by user_id, request_id when populated, and related_event_id. Used only participant-facing dataset files and the challenge contract; no organizer-only material or example output labels were used as evidence. The first five images belong to public examples, not evaluation requests.

Text screening covered attempts to override rules or roles, force outputs, expose secrets, redirect payments, or induce payment through urgency and promised rewards. English and Indonesian prize messages were inspected semantically; Indonesian and Spanish text is not itself suspicious. Image inspection covered visible wording, totals, cash state, dates, and linkage. This is not pixel-forensic authentication, steganography analysis, or a guarantee against every possible injection. Embedded links and payment prompts were not followed.

"Large" below means the five largest evaluation requests **within each home currency** (25 requests total). Currency ranks do not compare purchasing power across currencies. Also show requested amount divided by current balance minus minimum balance where positive. That difference ignores future commitments and is **not amount_safe_to_pay**.

## Prompt injection versus financial amendments

No reviewed content explicitly told the model to ignore the challenge rules, assume a privileged role, leak secrets, or output a chosen affordability result. The two prize messages attempt to persuade a person to pay; they are suspicious financial solicitations, not demonstrated model-directed prompt injection.

Statements such as "Please use the revised date" (message_73, request_95), removal of ended income, and confirmation of a settled transaction are financial evidence the challenge explicitly permits. They should update supported facts without changing the rules. Likewise, ordinary urgent-repair wording, a membership price, a family transfer, or multilingual wording is insufficient evidence of a scam.

## Structural integrity and misleading cash-state risks

Checks found zero duplicate primary IDs in requests, profiles, events, messages, images, or payment options. Populated message/image request and event links resolve to the same user; populated event lifecycle links resolve to the same user. All 16 blank event amounts have an image. Each evaluation request has two to four options (58 have two, 165 have three, 27 have four). No message associated with an evaluation user is dated after that user's request date. These checks establish internal consistency only; they cannot prove authenticity or exclude coordinated edits across files.

Other interpretation traps are present and should not be confused with deliberate tampering:

- **Unrealized investments:** request_70/message_52, request_105/message_79, request_266/message_207. Market value is not cash; the structured state matters even when a message says value increased substantially.
- **Uncredited prizes:** request_93/message_71, request_170/message_134, request_268/message_209. Processing/verification does not establish available funds, but these messages do not demand an upfront fee. Do not classify every prize message as a scam.
- **Settled prizes:** request_101/message_75 and event_9420, request_115/message_88, request_129/message_99, request_143/message_110. These describe completed one-off proceeds; do not forecast them as recurring income or count them again on top of current cash.
- **Possible duplicate card charges:** request_138/message_106, request_156/message_121, request_198/message_157, request_211/message_164, request_234/message_183, request_252/message_197. A disputed extra charge is not an already-posted reversal; preserve actual cash impact while unresolved. Distinct event IDs do not by themselves prove distinct economic transactions.
- **Failed payments with a retry:** request_91/message_69, request_229/message_179, request_253/message_198, request_259/message_201. Failure is not cancellation of the underlying bill; reserve the supported retry once.
- **Own-account transfers:** request_33/message_23, request_57/message_41, request_171/message_135, request_261/message_202, request_273/message_213. Matching credit/debit entries are not salary. Reconcile account scope rather than manufacturing new income.
- **Payment-method language:** some requests ask about part payments when allows_partial_payment=false. For example request_26 asks whether to send part; request_179 is also false. Generic wording does not override explicit eligibility or the profile. An available installment offer also must satisfy the user's method and duration preferences.

## Boundaries for the eventual implementation

Keep extracted facts separate from source instructions. Retain message/image/event IDs and confidence for each fact. Resolve explicit amendments and settlements before older estimates; never promote a message's claimed authority into control over the agent. Parse dates, currency, gross/net, amount received, balance due, and tender/change explicitly. Preserve an uncertainty flag for the hospital and handwritten receipts and use the financially safer supported interpretation when unresolved. Derive financial eligibility deterministically from profiles, events, supplied options, dated FX, and the 90-day balance check. Suspicious context must not silently replace the challenge's output semantics with a separate fraud classifier.
